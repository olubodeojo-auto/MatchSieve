# Target Customer Personas & Psychographic Research Plan

**Status:** Hypotheses for validation, not verified interviews or established conversion facts. Updated 21 October 2026.  
**Current market hypothesis:** adult, English-speaking UK customers first, with an English Premier League focus; consider selected US, Canadian, European and Australian markets after country-level payment, rights, tax and advertising checks.  
**Execution detail:** see [Paying-Customer Playbook](PAYING_CUSTOMER_PLAYBOOK.md), [Copy Bank](COPY_BANK.md) and [Conversion Experiments](CONVERSION_EXPERIMENTS.md).

## Product truth before persona promises

MatchSieve is a football research desk, not a livescore replacement or a promise of winning bets. Its defensible paid value must come from organising available data around a user's chosen fixture and market question, showing evidence on both sides, preserving sources and exposing missing inputs. The customer should be able to understand what a report can and cannot tell them.

No persona insight below should be treated as fact until it is validated by observed behaviour and interviews.

## Persona A — The Weekend Analyst (initial consumer beachhead)

### Situation and job
- **Who:** an adult, English-speaking football researcher who regularly looks into fixtures, likely EPL-focused at launch.
- **Typical moment:** researching several weekend fixtures, comparing goal or result markets, or trying to reconcile different pages before a match.
- **Current workaround:** opens multiple score/stat pages, manually checks form or H2H, compares contradictory claims and tries to remember where each fact came from.
- **Functional job:** organise the evidence relevant to the selected market without repeating the same research process across many tabs.
- **Emotional job:** feel prepared and in control of the research process—not rushed into a choice or persuaded by loud claims.
- **Identity:** wants to think of themselves as careful and independent, not someone who blindly copies tips.

### Psychographic hypotheses
- Values preparation, relevance, transparency and self-directed judgement.
- Has low patience for generic W/D/L strips, cluttered dashboards and confident language with no method.
- May be willing to pay when a report visibly does work they currently repeat. This is an assumption to test, not a claim about all football fans.
- May distrust AI-generated conclusions unless sources, sample sizes, missing inputs and counter-evidence are visible.

### Paying triggers to test
1. They have several fixtures to research in one sitting.
2. They use a sample report and can point to a specific section that saves them repeated cross-checking.
3. They return in another match week and choose another market.
4. They want a saved report/history rather than reconstructing the same research later.

**Best first message:** “One match. One market question. The evidence in one place.”  
**CTA to test:** “Explore a sample report” on marketing pages; “Research a match” in the working product.

### Objections and how to answer honestly
- **“I can get this for free.”** Agree that scores and basic statistics are free; explain the paid difference as market-specific organisation, counter-evidence, provenance and readable gaps.
- **“How do I know it is current?”** Show source and capture times when available; make stale-data rules visible. Do not say “live” when the underlying feed is not live or has not been tested.
- **“Can AI make this up?”** Structured facts and source records should be inspectable; optional commentary must not replace evidence or invent facts.
- **“Why pay every month?”** Show the report and let the user decide whether they repeat the workflow often enough. Do not push subscription on an occasional user who gets no recurring value.

## Persona B — The Disciplined Market Comparator

### Situation and job
- **Who:** an adult user who compares market types, team context and competing interpretations rather than copying a single tipster.
- **Typical moment:** two analysts disagree, a short trend seems to conflict with longer-term records, or a confident market claim needs scrutiny.
- **Functional job:** check whether an argument is supported by the available evidence and understand where it weakens.
- **Emotional job:** preserve independence and avoid feeling manipulated by selective winning records or exaggerated certainty.
- **Identity:** sceptical of hype; wants to make their own judgement with evidence in view.

### Psychographic hypotheses
- Values intellectual honesty, consistency, auditability and acknowledgement of unknowns.
- May scrutinise the report more deeply than Persona A.
- Price sensitivity and expectations of free access are unknown. Do not label this segment “non-paying” without evidence.
- May leave quickly if MatchSieve looks like a disguised tipster page or shows an opaque confidence meter.

### Paying triggers to test
- Uses the counter-evidence section to challenge an argument.
- Opens sources or checks the underlying record.
- Returns to compare another market or reviews the history of prior research.
- Sees a transparent record of limitations and later outcomes, not a selectively published win streak.

**Best first message:** “More opinions are not the same as more evidence.”  
**CTA to test:** “Inspect the evidence.”

**Safety guardrail:** do not target people trying to recover losses, imply research can solve financial problems or pressure users to stake more or act faster.

## Persona C — The Football Publisher (potential high-value segment)

### Situation and job
- **Who:** an adult content creator, community administrator, podcast host, independent preview writer or football publisher who researches repeatedly.
- **Typical moment:** preparing a match preview, recording a show, planning a discussion thread, creating a newsletter or preparing several fixture summaries before a deadline.
- **Functional job:** establish a repeatable research base with traceable source notes and evidence on both sides.
- **Emotional job:** protect credibility, reduce last-minute scrambling and feel prepared for questions from their audience.
- **Identity:** wants to be seen as thorough and useful—not a reposting channel for generic picks.

### Psychographic hypotheses
- Values repeatability, speed, clear provenance, editorial control and output that can be reused in a workflow.
- May have a stronger repeated-use reason than occasional researchers because new content is created each week.
- Could become a distribution route if they genuinely find the report useful, but creator reach does not guarantee referrals or paid conversion.
- Will not pay for export or workflow features that do not match their actual process. Interview before building a full creator suite.

### Paying triggers to test
1. A real upcoming publishing deadline creates an immediate research job.
2. The user reuses the report structure for multiple fixtures.
3. Source notes help them verify a claim while drafting or editing.
4. They return across multiple weeks and can name the step they no longer need to do manually.
5. A real paid pilot is completed and renewed.

**Best first message:** “Research the match. Keep the sources. Make the argument yours.”  
**CTA to test:** “Review a sample research brief.”

**Potential later tier, only after validation:** higher report allowance, multi-fixture workflow, source-preserving export and publisher archive. Validate display/republication permissions before describing the product as suitable for commercial republication.

## Segment using behaviour, not unsupported demographic assumptions

Track the job and observed behaviour rather than guessing age, income, personality, or gambling propensity:
- research frequency: occasional / weekly / multiple sessions per week;
- task: individual research / comparison / publishing;
- fixtures handled per session;
- evidence behaviour: reads only summary / opens counter-evidence / inspects sources;
- repeat behaviour: first report / second report later in week / returns in later match week;
- stated blocker: trust / unclear difference / price / missing coverage / workflow fit.

Use adult-focused research and marketing. Do not infer financial hardship, loss-chasing or other sensitive states for targeting.

## Anti-personas and disqualification signals

MatchSieve should not be positioned to serve:
- minors;
- people seeking guaranteed picks or risk-free betting outcomes;
- people explicitly trying to recover gambling losses or solve a financial crisis through betting;
- users expecting unlimited live scores without a paid plan or licensed data access;
- users demanding a forced daily quota when the evidence is insufficient.

If interviews reveal a desire for “sure wins”, do not treat it as a copy problem to exploit. Clarify the product's research purpose and decline that promise.

## Interview script (8–12 adults for the first qualitative round)

Do not reveal the proposed price or preferred solution until after the first five questions.

1. Think about the last match you researched. Take me through exactly what you did.
2. Which pages/apps did you use and why?
3. Which step took the longest or was hardest to verify?
4. Tell me about the last time two sources contradicted each other.
5. What information did you want but could not find or interpret easily?
6. Do you currently pay for any football data, content, community or analysis service? What made it worth—or not worth—the money?
7. What would make you distrust a research report?
8. Show a real, reviewed MatchSieve sample. What is useful, confusing, missing or unbelievable?
9. What would have to change in your workflow for you to use this next week?
10. Would you prefer a defined report-credit pack or monthly pass? Why? Treat the answer as a hypothesis.
11. When the checkout and product are genuinely ready, offer a real paid pilot. Observe the action, not only the stated intention.
12. For publishers: show how they currently prepare one preview; ask which source record or output format would fit their process.

Record consented notes, anonymise names, and separate verbatim statements from interpretation. Do not call interview statements purchase intent.

## Interview scorecard

Rate each from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), recording a quote or observed action for every score:

| Dimension | Evidence to record |
|---|---|
| Pain frequency | How many real research sessions occur in a normal week? |
| Workaround cost | Actual steps, tools and time spent on the last job |
| Product differentiation | Can they explain the paid difference from free score/stat pages? |
| Trust | Can they inspect a claim's source and name a limitation? |
| Repeat potential | Do they have a reason to do another report next week? |
| Payment evidence | Paid purchase/renewal; otherwise mark as unproven |
| Segment fit | Does the current product serve the stated job without unbuilt features? |

A segment score is a prioritisation aid, not statistical proof. Payment evidence and repeat behaviour should outweigh flattering feedback.

## Decision rules

- Keep Persona A as the initial consumer beachhead if the product repeatedly solves the fixture-to-market research task with low friction.
- Test Persona C early because a recurring publishing workflow may create repeat usage and support a future professional tier. Do not assume this without paid-pilot evidence.
- Keep Persona B in research until there is evidence they do not pay. Their scepticism may be addressed by transparency, but their willingness to pay must be measured.
- If users cannot explain the paid difference after seeing the report, improve the product/report and message before buying traffic.
