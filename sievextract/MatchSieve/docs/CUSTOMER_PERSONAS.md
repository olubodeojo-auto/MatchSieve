# Target Customer Personas & Psychographic Research Plan

**Status:** Initial hypotheses for validation, updated 20 October 2026. These are not verified interviews or established conversion facts. The current market hypothesis is UK first at a £7–£10/month test price, then selective US/Canada/Europe/Australia expansion after compliance checks.

## Persona A — The Weekend Analyst (beachhead)

- **Who:** adult, English-speaking football researcher in the UK as the first market hypothesis (later: selected US/Canada/European/Australian audiences), likely EPL-focused, who researches fixtures regularly and wants to compare a particular market.
- **Behaviour:** opens several match/stat pages, checks recent results, goal tendencies and team news, may compare odds, often repeats the workflow for many fixtures.
- **Functional pain:** repeated page switching, copying numbers, uncertainty about whether information is current, and a poor way to compare evidence for a market.
- **Emotional pain:** “I have looked at five pages and I still don't know which facts matter.” They want to feel prepared rather than rushed or fooled.
- **Desired job:** organise the evidence quickly so they can make their own judgement.
- **Trust builders:** sources and timestamps; opposing evidence; clear sample size; distinction between historical figures and current news; visible missing data; historical record including misses.
- **Payment trigger to test:** a sample report saves obvious effort and answers the actual market question they came with.
- **Objections:** “I can get this free”; “AI will just make things up”; “How do I know it is current?”; “Why should I pay weekly?”
- **Best hook to test:** “Choose the match. Choose your market. We’ll bring the evidence into one clear report.”
- **Product features:** market selector, evidence report, source trail, current-context warnings, save/share/export later, repeat-research archive.

## Persona B — The Disciplined Market Comparator

- **Who:** adult user who compares market types and prediction sources rather than copying one tipster. Price sensitivity and expectation of free access are open questions to validate, not proven traits of this persona.
- **Behaviour:** checks odds, 1X2 and goal markets, compares several opinions, asks “why this market?”
- **Pain:** conflicting claims, unsupported certainty, selectively published wins, and unclear differences between historical hit-rate and probability.
- **Emotional job:** regain a sense of control and avoid feeling manipulated by tip sellers.
- **Payment trigger:** a transparent report that includes counter-evidence and a reliable results ledger.
- **Objection:** “All prediction pages say they are accurate.”
- **Hook:** “Don't just read the pick. See what supports it, what challenges it and what is still unknown.”
- **Guardrail:** Never target someone trying to recover losses; never encourage higher stakes to chase losses; make uncertainty prominent.

## Persona C — The Football Publisher

- **Who:** adult content creator, community admin, podcaster or preview writer who needs reliable research for recurring content.
- **Behaviour:** prepares several preview posts or match discussions every week, needs credible stats and repeatable report structure.
- **Pain:** researching from scratch and risk of sharing outdated or mislabelled facts.
- **Emotional job:** protect credibility and use more time creating useful content.
- **Payment trigger:** repeatable, exportable research with traceable sources.
- **Objection:** “Can I get the research in a format that fits my workflow?”
- **Hook:** “Spend less time hunting for match facts and more time explaining the game.”
- **Potential later tier:** professional archive/export plan or higher-volume creator tier after proof of demand, not the first launch. Treat this segment as a possible distribution and retention advantage—not a guarantee that advertising will be unnecessary.

## Interview script (10–15 target users)

Do not reveal the proposed price/solution until after asking the first five questions.

1. Think about the last match you researched. Take me through what you did.
2. Which pages/apps did you use and why?
3. What took the longest or was hardest to verify?
4. Tell me about the last time two sources contradicted each other.
5. What information did you wish you had but couldn't find easily?
6. Do you currently pay for any football data, community, content or analysis service? What made it worth—or not worth—the money?
7. What would make you distrust a research report?
8. Show a sample MatchSieve report. What is useful, confusing, missing or unbelievable?
9. Would you prefer to pay per market report, buy a small weekly credit pack, or subscribe monthly? Why?
10. Offer a real test purchase (not just a survey intention) where the checkout and product are ready. Record what the person does, not merely what they say.

Record consented notes, anonymise names, and separate verbatim statements from the team's interpretation. Score segment pain, frequency, current workaround, willingness to pay and repeat-use potential. Interviewees are not statistically representative; the goal is to find language and buying behaviour worth testing.
